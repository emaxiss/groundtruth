# Refund Policy

This policy is the single source of truth for refunds. Support agents cannot grant exceptions outside these terms.

## Annual plans

Annual subscriptions are eligible for a full refund within 14 calendar days of the initial charge. The 14-day window is measured from the charge timestamp, inclusive: a request submitted on day 14 qualifies; a request on day 15 does not.

After 14 days, annual plans are not refundable. The subscription remains active until the end of the paid term, and the workspace can cancel auto-renewal at any time from Settings > Billing.

## Monthly plans

Monthly subscriptions are refundable only within 48 hours of the charge. The 48-hour window is measured from the charge timestamp, inclusive: a request at exactly 48 hours qualifies; a request at 49 hours does not.

After 48 hours, monthly charges are not refunded, including partial-month refunds for unused time. Cancelling a monthly plan stops future charges; it does not refund the current period.

## Downgrades

Downgrades are never refunded as cash. Downgrading from Team to Pro, or Pro to Free, takes effect at the end of the current billing period. Any prorated difference is issued as account credit applied to future invoices, not as a refund to the original payment method.

## Seat reductions

Removing seats mid-period generates prorated account credit, not a refund. Credit is applied automatically to the next invoice and does not expire.

## Duplicate and erroneous charges

Duplicate charges and charges caused by a TaskLoop billing error are refunded in full regardless of the windows above. These require verification by the billing team and are typically processed within 5 business days.

## Processing

Approved refunds are returned to the original payment method within 5-10 business days depending on the card issuer. TaskLoop does not refund to a different payment method, issue cash equivalents, or provide refunds for time on the Free plan.

## Escalation

Requests outside these terms are declined. Agents must route disputed cases to the billing team rather than promising an outcome.
